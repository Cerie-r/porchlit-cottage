# Resident motion · first living-room pass

The accepted wardrobe and avatar geometry remain the source of both residents. `avatar-rig.js` binds the visible meshes to a shared seven-joint skeleton without removing any body or garment triangles. The wardrobe itself is unchanged and stays unrigged. Motion stops requesting frames when a pose settles, and pauses beneath overlays and in hidden tabs.

In the first-floor overview or living room, the wardrobe’s “我” character is controlled (girl by default); tap open ground to walk. Tapping the companion does not transfer control. Swap “我／小机” in the wardrobe to change the controlled role. Drag/pinch remain camera gestures. Tap a seat anywhere to open a compact nearby “坐下／翻找” menu. The computer chair has only “坐下” because it has no storage. Selection or a ground/canvas press dismisses the menu. No sofa action toolbar is shown. Living armchair, upstairs reading chairs and bedroom bench use the same physical-seat targets. Cabinet storage opens directly. Walking and turning are 1.5 times faster. A seated resident also stands before following a new floor destination. Seats are separate for the two residents and follow the sofa's saved rotation and position. Reopening a room does not replay the previous seat command.

Routes use a bounded grid with continuous segment checks and no diagonal corner cutting. Obstacles follow the major living-room furniture and the other resident; blocked destinations are rejected. Rearranging furniture resets motion to a nearby clear point. Movement supports the living room, kitchen, study and bedroom, with major-furniture obstacles in each room. Wandering, carrying objects and person-to-person interactions are future work. Small freely placed creations are not navigation obstacles yet. Movement positions are session-local and do not overwrite wardrobe or house layout saves.

Verified with tests for routing to both sofa seats, impossible routes, interruption while sitting, settled frame state, and rest-geometry/skin-weight invariants. Offline mesh snapshots inspected for stand, stride and sit on both accepted outfits. Browser/touch QA was unavailable in this environment; inspect actual seat contact and mobile gestures next.

Seat targets cancel the stage transform into room coordinates, then subtract the resident navigation origin. Stage orbit, zoom, pan and focused-room placement cannot affect sit/approach positions or seat height. Seated-to-seat commands stand before following the new path. Upstairs obstacles are conservative major-furniture footprints; smaller decorations remain outside navigation.

The sidebar floor/room selects one keyed resident group, including the kitchen. Switching rooms unmounts the previous pair, cancels the old route/pose and spawns both residents in the current room. Room-local spawn points avoid major furniture and the companion. There is no second pair retained on another floor. Regression checks include a rendered Three.js furniture hierarchy under rotated/scaled/panned stages.

Seat contact is measured from the fully seated, preserved body surface. The root subtracts the contact height and facing-dependent depth so the pelvis touches the cushion rather than aligning its hip joint with the cushion. The computer chair model and interaction share one cushion layout. Seat and camera action labels project from their furniture anchors each frame requested by camera or stage movement. The book stays available in the top “布置” control as a miniature book, with its panel opened on demand. Camera action bars hide during book editing. Offline chair/avatar meshes were inspected from front and rear; real browser/touch QA remains unavailable.

Bedroom tea-table and keepsake placements now share `bedroom-corner.js`; the keepsake is moved clear of the tea table and both footprints block navigation. Living furniture footprints follow actual sofa/armchair edges with a .17 body radius, keeping the nearby sofa-to-chair aisle open; the regression route is about 2 units rather than 7.7. First-floor overview uses combined living/kitchen coordinates and obstacles; kitchen-only bounds include the dining chairs. All three dining chairs and both counter stools have seat targets. Dining chairs approach from the side to avoid walking through their backrests. The third chair faces the table. The faucet toggles visible water, stove controls toggle illuminated burners, and the oven door opens onto a hollow cavity with a rack. These are local scene interactions, not a cooking/inventory system. Existing fridge/cabinet searching remains separate.

Furniture label projection is viewport-clamped and tested under orbit and zoom; it replaces bottom-fixed action bars. The lower floating book launcher is replaced by the top toolbar portal, leaving the room floor unobstructed.
